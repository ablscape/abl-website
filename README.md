# ABL Landscape Architecture website

The source of ablscape.com. Plain HTML and CSS, generated by one script from a text file per project. Nothing to install and nothing to update.

- **Editing the site:** see [EDITOR-GUIDE.md](EDITOR-GUIDE.md). Changes are made by asking Claude.
- **Rules Claude follows:** see [CLAUDE.md](CLAUDE.md). Claude reads it at the start of every session.

## How it fits together

| Piece | Job |
|---|---|
| This repository (GitHub) | The single source of the site. Every change is a pull request. |
| Netlify | Builds a preview for each pull request and publishes `main` to the live site. |
| Claude | Edits the files in `content/`, opens the pull request, merges on "publish". |
| GoDaddy | Domain registration, DNS and Microsoft 365 email. The website needs two DNS records there and nothing else. |

## Layout

```
content/
  site.json              firm details, home page, Capabilities page (with the firm's introduction), Process page, contact form choices
  people.json            the People page
  legacy-urls.json       addresses from the old site, forwarded to new pages
  projects/<slug>/
    project.json         one project: facts, story, photo list
    photos/              01-640.jpg, 01-1280.jpg, 01-1920.jpg, 01-2560.jpg ...
static/                  copied as is: stylesheet, script, fonts, logo, portraits
templates/               one file per kind of page
lib/                     loading, checking, image sizes
build.js                 the builder
tools/prepare_photos.py  resizes photographs, converts to sRGB, strips location data
netlify.toml             build settings
```

## Building

Needs Node 18 or later. No `npm install`.

```
node build.js               # preview view: drafts and gray notes included
node build.js --production  # live-site view: published projects only
node build.js --check       # list everything unfinished
node build.js --portable    # preview/ folder that opens from disk, for sending to someone
node build.js --no-notes    # drafts included, notes left out: the design as it will look
```

The build stops with a plain-language list if content is malformed, a photograph is missing, or a link leads nowhere.

`netlify.toml` builds the live site with `--production`, so drafts and preview notes cannot reach it. Pull request previews use the plain build and show them.

## Design settings

Colors, typefaces and the four text sizes are variables in the first block of `static/assets/css/site.css`. Every rule takes its size from one of the four names, and a heading is never smaller than the text it stands over. The page is white (`--paper`, with the same color as three numbers in `--paper-rgb`): a pale gray-green page was tried in rounds 16 and 17, and the firm asked three times for it whiter. On it stand near-black type, the green of the logo's "abl" (`--sage`), which is also the low band at the foot of every page (a darker green stood there until round 19, when the firm asked for the logo's own; in round 23 the firm asked for a smaller footer with the essentials alone, so the band is lower and carries the "abl", the firm's name and address, and its telephone and emails; the five links that repeated the header are gone), and one warm accent (terracotta) for short rules, numerals and links on hover. If the page is ever toned, never cream or yellow. Preview notes are gray, so that they lend the pages no color while the design is judged; they never appear on the live site. No gray on the site is warm. The typeface is Barlow throughout, in four weights, self-hosted under the SIL Open Font License; the firm may name the typeface from its drawings to replace it.

The firm's hand drawings are the theme of the site, and its restraint is deliberate: no video, no movement or effect that draws attention to itself. A plan runs across the home page under the firm's statement, and a drawing stands behind the heading of the Work, People, Process and Contact pages (`backdrop` in `content/site.json`; Capabilities has none since the firm rejected the Children's Garden plan there in round 23 and said it would send another), three stand in a row lower on the home page (`drawings`), and a project may lead with a drawing (`cover`). A drawing behind a heading is shown as ink on the page: it has no frame and no band, a drawing made on gray paper has its paper brought to white first (`lift`), and it dissolves into the page above, below and toward the text. Behind a heading the drawing is shown whole and is never enlarged to fill its block, since the files are small and a drawing stretched across the window looks soft: on a wide screen it stands at the right, as tall as the block and as wide as its own proportions make it (`--ratio`), with the text at the left; on a phone or a tablet the text comes first and the whole drawing follows across the page. `shift` in a drawing's entry moves it down on a wide screen so that a line of it meets a line of the text: the Work page's plan stands 2.75 rem lower since round 23, when the firm asked that its lower line of trees end level with the last line of the introduction (the header's lightening and the clearing around the logo stay where the header is). Nothing moves. A drawing behind a heading is turned a few degrees from yellow toward green (`--plan-key` in the stylesheet) unless its entry says `asDrawn`. The same drawing keeps its own colors on its project page and wherever else it appears.

Every drawing takes the sheet as its paper (`[data-sheet]` in the stylesheet: the drawing is multiplied into the page's color), so it reads as ink on the page with no rectangle around it. A drawing stands where a page begins, behind its heading, and a project shows its own plans between its opening and its photographs; a plan can stand beside the photograph of what was built from it (`built` in the drawing's entry). Process keeps one drawing, at its head, and the Dialogue diagram, which is centered under the text it follows (round 23). Tried and taken out: a page-wide ground of traced linework (round 14), which turned a drawing into a pattern, and a drawing at the foot of every page (round 15), which read as decoration.

Projects are listed two to a line: the picture, then the kind of project, its name, and its place. On the Work page the list is wider than the page's text, with a little space at the outer edges of the window and a narrow space between the pictures; under "Selected work" on the home page it stays inside the page's margins (the firm asked for the Work page's pictures larger in round 18 and for the home page's a little smaller in round 20). One project alone from edge to edge was tried in round 16 and read as too large and "too zoomed in", the more so because most of the files are 1,280 pixels wide. Every photograph on a project page, the lead photograph included, keeps the margins and its own proportions. Under the lead photograph the project's title stands alone across the page, with the summary beneath it a size larger than running text, and the facts and the text begin on one line under them, the facts in the first four columns; until round 23 the title shared the facts' column and the text beside it began above the title, which the firm found slight and awkward ("this should be more prominent and it looks weird with the other text to the right that's above it"). A computer rendering listed among a project's drawings (`"rendering": true`) stands after the photographs, two to a line across three quarters of the page, since the firm asked in round 21 that photographs be more prominent than renderings; the Virginia Horse Center's aerial rendering was trimmed of the white fade at its edges. On a computer no photograph on a project page crosses the whole window; the home page's photographs do, in a low band. `main` clips sideways overflow, so that nothing reaching past the margins can make the window scroll sideways.

One photograph on the site is retouched. In round 23, at the firm's request, the passing car was painted out of the first photograph on the VMI page (`content/projects/virginia-military-institute/photos/01-*.jpg`): the stretch of wall, curb and road it hid is laid in from the wall, curb and road beside it in the same photograph, so that stretch is a reconstruction, and nothing else in the photograph was changed. The untouched files are in the repository's history, in the commit before the change.

Spacing and alignment follow a few rules, checked across the site in round 23 at the firm's request. On a computer every page is set on the same twelve columns: titles, names and labels in the first four, running text from the fifth, on every page (a project's text started in the sixth until then). The title of a page stands at the same height on every page (`.page-statement { align-items: start }`); every hairline keeps the page's margins (the line above "Next project" ran to the edge of the page box); every page leaves the same space above the footer, the last block having no bottom padding of its own; and a page shorter than the window fills it (`body` is a column as tall as the window, `main` takes the room). The contact form has no box around it. Text is typeset as it is written out (`typeset()` in `lib/html.js`): apostrophes and paired double quotation marks become typographic ones.

The home page opens with photographs of built work across the whole width of the window, which follow one another. On a computer they stand in a low band, 15 to 4 and never more than 46 percent of the window's height, so each two-to-one photograph loses about half its height between its head and foot (`focus` on a slide in `hero` says where its subject sits); the line that names the project keeps the page's margins. The arrangement was reached in five steps: across the whole window at two to one the firm found the photographs "way too big" (rounds 18 and 19); beside the statement, in seven of the page's twelve columns, it did not like them as the opening of the site (rounds 20 and 21); across the top inside the page's margins they did not reach far enough ("this main thing should span the full horizontal"); in round 22 they spanned the window in a band of 14 to 5; and in round 23 the band was lowered, because the firm did not like "how far down it goes" and asked for the photograph to be cropped to the width, not enlarged to fit. A short account of the firm follows, with its name at the left and the paragraph at the right; it stood at the foot of the page with links to People and Process until the firm found it hidden there and the links a repeat of the header, beside the plan in rounds 20 and 21, and under the statement in round 22. Then one of the firm's plans (`backdrop`) runs across the whole width of the window as a band, and the firm's statement is set over it at the left, as the firm asked in round 23 ("make it span horizontally and have the abl ... text superimposed on it"); the statement stood beside the plan until then. The band is as tall as the plan's own proportions make it and never taller than four fifths of the window, so the whole plan shows on most screens and is trimmed a little at its head and foot on a low window; it was first a low band that showed three fifths of the plan's height, and the firm asked for it larger ("this space can be larger so you don't need to zoom in so much on the background draft diagram"). The statement stands in the upper left, over the plan's open ground and clear of the theater and its parking. The plan is lightened under the words by its own overlay, with no box, and faded into the page at its head and foot, so that the band has no edge. The band's height is written out (`min(100vw / ratio, 80vh)`), since `aspect-ratio` with a maximum height would narrow the band. Its file is 1,280 pixels wide, so across a wide window it is enlarged and a little soft. Selected work and a row of three plans follow. On a tablet or a phone the photographs run from edge to edge at two to one and three to two, and the statement comes first with the whole plan after it.

The Process page sets every part of the firm's process the same way: a hairline, the part's number and name at the left, its text at the right, in one column down the page. A remark stands under the part's name, at the left, beside the text it belongs to (it stood under the text until round 22, where the firm found it lonely); the Client, Site, Design team diagram or a picture stands under the text where a part has one. Until round 20 each part had a layout of its own, and the firm found the page cumbersome.

Capabilities is a page of its own since round 23, with its link before Process; it closed the Process page until the firm asked for the change ("i feel like cababilities shouldn't be under process but its own tab before process"). Its heading carries the firm's one sentence on what it does; the Children's Garden plan stood beside it until the firm rejected that drawing, and the heading is plain until the firm sends another. Each capability is then set as a part of the Process page is: a hairline, the name at the left, and at the right a sentence explaining what the capability is. The page first listed under each capability the projects that show it, and closed with the firm's qualifications; later in round 23 the firm asked for explanations in place of the examples and for the qualifications to come off ("rather than have examples, I think we should provide an explanation of like what those services are"). A capability stands by its name alone until the firm has approved its sentence. Between the heading and the capabilities stands the firm's own introduction, its first paragraph a size larger, with the line on its certification. What the firm does for private properties follows in the same form.

The header has the five links (Work, People, Capabilities, Process, Contact) at the left and the logo at the right (the logo stood at the left in round 18, and the firm asked for it back). The heading of each page is a low block with the title and a short opening at the left and a drawing at the right. On a wide screen the drawing runs up behind the header to the top of the window (`--head-h` is the header's height), so the page begins with the drawing; there it is lightened, and cleared where the logo stands, by the drawing's own overlay, so the header reads over it with no band and no box. The line that names the drawing stands at the foot of the drawing, at the outer edge of the window (it stood on the header's right edge until round 23, when the firm asked for it further right on the People page, clear of the text beneath the heading); the drawing has sunk into the page before it. A heading carries its title and at most a short opening. The People page is its heading and the people: the firm's introduction and the line on its certification stood under that heading until round 23, when the firm said they belonged under Capabilities, and they follow that page's heading now (`capabilities.intro` and `capabilities.certification`; the home page's account of the firm is the first paragraph of the same text). A heading with no drawing beside it leaves no room for one, so what follows stands closer under it.

Nothing changes under the pointer. In round 20 the header turned white under the pointer, which the firm had asked for after the Design Workshop site; in round 22 the firm asked for it gone ("i like it more the way it was"), and the bar, its rules and its script were removed. In the same round the firm asked for a little more fading around the logo where it stands over a drawing: the clearing in the drawing's own overlay (`.page-statement.has-plan .statement-plan::after`) is an ellipse of 14.5 by 5 rem, and the drawing behind the header is lightened by three fifths of the page's color at the top of the window.

The "abl" of the logo never leaves the window. Once the script has run (the class `mark-ready` on the root), a mark fixed to the window draws the "abl" exactly where the logo's own stands and at its size, and the logo in the header is clipped to its two words; the header scrolls away and the letters stay. Until round 20 the mark faded in after the header had gone, and the firm found that it took several scrolls to show. The mark is green over the page and over drawings and white over photographs, and the links return where they stood when the visitor scrolls up (in a window narrower than 640 pixels the logo stands above the links, the mark is at the top left, and the links return beneath it, where they stood; five links did not fit beside the mark, where four had returned until round 23). The stylesheet derives the mark from the logo: `--mark-w` and `--mark-top` are computed from `--logo-w` and `--head-pad`, `--edge-box` places the mark and the links from the window's edge without counting a scrollbar, the way the header is placed, and `--mark-shift`, set by the script, keeps the mark on the logo where a preview build's bar stands above the header. Measured in Chrome and WebKit at sixteen window widths, with and without a scrollbar that takes room, the mark lies within a fiftieth of a pixel of the logo's letters. Where it is green the mark carries a soft white haze, so that text or linework passing beneath fades out around the letters; a picture marked `data-photo` or `data-sheet` in the templates carries its own copy, clipped to its frame. The returning links are drawn the way the mark is: dark, in medium weight, on a patch of white haze over the page and over drawings; white with a close dark shadow over photographs. A page reached by a link always opens at its top, whatever the browser or the window showing the site does with the old position (a script in the head of every page sees to it; going back or forward keeps the browser's own place). The mark is a vector redrawn from the 230-pixel logo and fitted to it by measurement (`MARK_PATH` in `lib/html.js`). The header logo, `static/assets/img/logo.svg`, is a drawing too: the same mark, with the two words set in Crimson Text (an open-licensed typeface chosen as the nearest of ten compared against the old file) and converted to outlines, so it stays sharp on every screen and loads no font.

## Before launch

- [ ] Replace the redrawn logo with the firm's own artwork (AI, EPS, PDF or SVG from whoever designed it). `static/assets/img/logo.svg` is a redrawing; `logo.jpg` beside it is the 230-pixel file from the old site that it was drawn from, kept for reference.
- [ ] Rescan the hand drawings at full size and prepare them again: those on the site are 1280 pixels wide, and they stand behind the headings and, in the Lime Kiln plan's case, across the whole home page, where a large screen shows them soft.
- [ ] `node build.js --check` reports nothing unfinished that matters.
- [ ] Turn on form detection in Netlify (Forms) and set the notification email for the `inquiry` form.
- [ ] Delete the `X-Robots-Tag` block in `netlify.toml`.
- [ ] In Netlify, add `www.ablscape.com` as the primary domain. At GoDaddy, edit the existing A record for `@` to Netlify's load balancer address and change the `www` CNAME to the Netlify site address. Netlify's domain panel shows the exact values. Leave the MX, autodiscover and TXT records untouched: they carry the firm's email.
- [ ] Keep the old GoDaddy hosting for a month as a fallback. Then cancel that product only, after confirming the Microsoft 365 email is billed separately.
