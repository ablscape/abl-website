# ABL Landscape Architecture website

The source of ablscape.com. Plain HTML and CSS, generated by one script from a text file per project. Nothing to install and nothing to update.

- **Editing the site:** see [EDITOR-GUIDE.md](EDITOR-GUIDE.md). Changes are made by asking Claude.
- **Rules Claude follows:** see [CLAUDE.md](CLAUDE.md). Claude reads it at the start of every session.

## How it fits together

| Piece | Job |
|---|---|
| This repository (GitHub) | The single source of the site. Every change is a pull request. |
| Netlify | Builds a preview for each pull request and publishes `main` to the live site. |
| Claude | Edits the files in `content/`, opens the pull request, merges on "publish". |
| GoDaddy | Domain registration, DNS and Microsoft 365 email. The website needs two DNS records there and nothing else. |

## Layout

```
content/
  site.json              firm details, home page, People page text, Process page, contact form choices
  people.json            the People page
  legacy-urls.json       addresses from the old site, forwarded to new pages
  projects/<slug>/
    project.json         one project: facts, story, photo list
    photos/              01-640.jpg, 01-1280.jpg, 01-1920.jpg, 01-2560.jpg ...
static/                  copied as is: stylesheet, script, fonts, logo, portraits
templates/               one file per kind of page
lib/                     loading, checking, image sizes
build.js                 the builder
tools/prepare_photos.py  resizes photographs, converts to sRGB, strips location data
netlify.toml             build settings
```

## Building

Needs Node 18 or later. No `npm install`.

```
node build.js               # preview view: drafts and gray notes included
node build.js --production  # live-site view: published projects only
node build.js --check       # list everything unfinished
node build.js --portable    # preview/ folder that opens from disk, for sending to someone
node build.js --no-notes    # drafts included, notes left out: the design as it will look
```

The build stops with a plain-language list if content is malformed, a photograph is missing, or a link leads nowhere.

`netlify.toml` builds the live site with `--production`, so drafts and preview notes cannot reach it. Pull request previews use the plain build and show them.

## Design settings

Colors, typefaces and the four text sizes are variables in the first block of `static/assets/css/site.css`. Every rule takes its size from one of the four names, and a heading is never smaller than the text it stands over. The page is white (`--paper`, with the same color as three numbers in `--paper-rgb`): a pale gray-green page was tried in rounds 16 and 17, and the firm asked three times for it whiter. On it stand near-black type, the green of the logo's "abl" (`--sage`), which is also the block at the foot of every page (a darker green stood there until round 19, when the firm asked for the logo's own), and one warm accent (terracotta) for short rules, numerals and links on hover. If the page is ever toned, never cream or yellow. Preview notes are gray, so that they lend the pages no color while the design is judged; they never appear on the live site. No gray on the site is warm. The typeface is Barlow throughout, in four weights, self-hosted under the SIL Open Font License; the firm may name the typeface from its drawings to replace it.

The firm's hand drawings are the theme of the site, and its restraint is deliberate: no video, no movement or effect that draws attention to itself. A drawing lies beside the statement on the home page and behind the heading of the Work, People, Process and Contact pages (`backdrop` in `content/site.json`), three stand in a row lower on the home page (`drawings`), and a project may lead with a drawing (`cover`). A drawing behind a heading is shown as ink on the page: it has no frame and no band, a drawing made on gray paper has its paper brought to white first (`lift`), and it dissolves into the page above, below and toward the text. The drawing is shown whole and is never enlarged to fill its block, since the files are small and a drawing stretched across the window looks soft: on a wide screen it stands at the right, as tall as the block and as wide as its own proportions make it (`--ratio`), with the text at the left; on a phone or a tablet the text comes first and the whole drawing follows across the page. Nothing moves. A drawing behind a heading is turned a few degrees from yellow toward green (`--plan-key` in the stylesheet) unless its entry says `asDrawn`. The same drawing keeps its own colors on its project page and wherever else it appears.

Every drawing takes the sheet as its paper (`[data-sheet]` in the stylesheet: the drawing is multiplied into the page's color), so it reads as ink on the page with no rectangle around it. A drawing stands where a page begins, behind its heading, and a project shows its own plans between its opening and its photographs; a plan can stand beside the photograph of what was built from it (`built` in the drawing's entry). Process keeps one drawing, at its head, and the Dialogue diagram. Tried and taken out: a page-wide ground of traced linework (round 14), which turned a drawing into a pattern, and a drawing at the foot of every page (round 15), which read as decoration.

Projects are listed two to a line: the picture, then the kind of project, its name, and its place. On the Work page the list is wider than the page's text, with a little space at the outer edges of the window and a narrow space between the pictures; under "Selected work" on the home page it stays inside the page's margins (the firm asked for the Work page's pictures larger in round 18 and for the home page's a little smaller in round 20). One project alone from edge to edge was tried in round 16 and read as too large and "too zoomed in", the more so because most of the files are 1,280 pixels wide. Every photograph on a project page, the lead photograph included, keeps the margins and its own proportions. A computer rendering listed among a project's drawings (`"rendering": true`) stands after the photographs, two to a line across three quarters of the page, since the firm asked in round 21 that photographs be more prominent than renderings; the Virginia Horse Center's aerial rendering was trimmed of the white fade at its edges. On a computer no photograph on a project page crosses the whole window; the home page's photographs do, in a low band. `main` clips sideways overflow, so that nothing reaching past the margins can make the window scroll sideways.

The home page opens with photographs of built work across the whole width of the window, which follow one another. On a computer they stand in a band lower than the photographs are, 14 to 5 and never more than 58 percent of the window's height, so each two-to-one photograph loses a part of its head and foot (`focus` on a slide in `hero` says where its subject sits); the line that names the project keeps the page's margins. The arrangement was reached in four steps: across the whole window at two to one the firm found the photographs "way too big" (rounds 18 and 19); beside the statement, in seven of the page's twelve columns, it did not like them as the opening of the site (rounds 20 and 21); across the top inside the page's margins they did not reach far enough ("this main thing should span the full horizontal"); so in round 22 they span the window in the low band. The firm's statement follows, beside one of the firm's plans (`backdrop`), where the firm asked for it; then a short account of the firm, with its name at the left and the paragraph at the right, which stood at the foot of the page with links to People and Process until the firm found it hidden there and the links a repeat of the header, and beside the plan in rounds 20 and 21; then Selected work and a row of three plans. On a tablet or a phone the photographs run from edge to edge at two to one and three to two.

The Process page sets every part of the firm's process the same way: a hairline, the part's number and name at the left, its text at the right, in one column down the page. A remark stands under the part's name, at the left, beside the text it belongs to (it stood under the text until round 22, where the firm found it lonely); the Client, Site, Design team diagram or a picture stands under the text where a part has one. Capabilities follows in the same form without a number. Until round 20 each part had a layout of its own, and the firm found the page cumbersome.

The header has the four links at the left and the logo at the right (the logo stood at the left in round 18, and the firm asked for it back). The heading of each page is a low block with the title and a short opening at the left and a drawing at the right. On a wide screen the drawing runs up behind the header to the top of the window (`--head-h` is the header's height), so the page begins with the drawing; there it is lightened, and cleared where the logo stands, by the drawing's own overlay, so the header reads over it with no band and no box. The line that names the drawing stands at the right, at the foot of the drawing, which has sunk into the page before it. A heading carries its title and at most a short opening; the People page's introduction stands below its heading.

Nothing changes under the pointer. In round 20 the header turned white under the pointer, which the firm had asked for after the Design Workshop site; in round 22 the firm asked for it gone ("i like it more the way it was"), and the bar, its rules and its script were removed. In the same round the firm asked for a little more fading around the logo where it stands over a drawing: the clearing in the drawing's own overlay (`.page-statement.has-plan .statement-plan::after`) is an ellipse of 14.5 by 5 rem, and the drawing behind the header is lightened by three fifths of the page's color at the top of the window.

The "abl" of the logo never leaves the window. Once the script has run (the class `mark-ready` on the root), a mark fixed to the window draws the "abl" exactly where the logo's own stands and at its size, and the logo in the header is clipped to its two words; the header scrolls away and the letters stay. Until round 20 the mark faded in after the header had gone, and the firm found that it took several scrolls to show. The mark is green over the page and over drawings and white over photographs, and the links return where they stood when the visitor scrolls up (on a phone the mark is at the top left and the links return at the top right). The stylesheet derives the mark from the logo: `--mark-w` and `--mark-top` are computed from `--logo-w` and `--head-pad`, `--edge-box` places the mark and the links from the window's edge without counting a scrollbar, the way the header is placed, and `--mark-shift`, set by the script, keeps the mark on the logo where a preview build's bar stands above the header. Measured in Chrome and WebKit at sixteen window widths, with and without a scrollbar that takes room, the mark lies within a fiftieth of a pixel of the logo's letters. Where it is green the mark carries a soft white haze, so that text or linework passing beneath fades out around the letters; a picture marked `data-photo` or `data-sheet` in the templates carries its own copy, clipped to its frame. The returning links are drawn the way the mark is: dark, in medium weight, on a patch of white haze over the page and over drawings; white with a close dark shadow over photographs. A page reached by a link always opens at its top, whatever the browser or the window showing the site does with the old position (a script in the head of every page sees to it; going back or forward keeps the browser's own place). The mark is a vector redrawn from the 230-pixel logo and fitted to it by measurement (`MARK_PATH` in `lib/html.js`). The header logo, `static/assets/img/logo.svg`, is a drawing too: the same mark, with the two words set in Crimson Text (an open-licensed typeface chosen as the nearest of ten compared against the old file) and converted to outlines, so it stays sharp on every screen and loads no font.

## Before launch

- [ ] Replace the redrawn logo with the firm's own artwork (AI, EPS, PDF or SVG from whoever designed it). `static/assets/img/logo.svg` is a redrawing; `logo.jpg` beside it is the 230-pixel file from the old site that it was drawn from, kept for reference.
- [ ] Rescan the hand drawings at full size and prepare them again: those on the site are 1280 pixels wide, and they stand behind every heading and at the foot of every page, where a large screen shows them soft.
- [ ] `node build.js --check` reports nothing unfinished that matters.
- [ ] Turn on form detection in Netlify (Forms) and set the notification email for the `inquiry` form.
- [ ] Delete the `X-Robots-Tag` block in `netlify.toml`.
- [ ] In Netlify, add `www.ablscape.com` as the primary domain. At GoDaddy, edit the existing A record for `@` to Netlify's load balancer address and change the `www` CNAME to the Netlify site address. Netlify's domain panel shows the exact values. Leave the MX, autodiscover and TXT records untouched: they carry the firm's email.
- [ ] Keep the old GoDaddy hosting for a month as a fallback. Then cancel that product only, after confirming the Microsoft 365 email is billed separately.
